# Solution 33: Apex Home Services (fictional worked example)

Apex Home Services: 30 staff, plumbing and HVAC, one office manager who doubles as IT, losing after-hours bookings, process unchanged for years, franchising ambitions in three years. Scores in `score_home_services.py`:

- **Buy 23/25.** Speed 4 (bookings leak nightly, evidence: the office voicemail fills every weekend). No internal builder 5 (the office manager is the IT department). Process stability 5 (same dispatch process for six years, and it is written in the ops binder). Standard shape 5 (capture, qualify, book, follow up, unchanged from Lesson 31). Failure is operational 4 (a missed agent means missed jobs, painful but recoverable).
- **Build 10/25.** Strategic capability 3, the interesting one: franchising in three years argues for owning the playbook eventually, which is why it is not a 1. Expertise 1, data sensitivity 2, integration depth 2 (standard calendar and invoicing tools), volume economics 2.

**Verdict: BUY, lead of 13.** The two factors doing the work are speed and the absent builder. Both are verifiably true today, not aspirational.

**The strongest case for the other side, honestly made:** Apex wants to franchise. A franchise sells a repeatable operating system, and an agent playbook it does not own or understand is a hole in that system. If Apex builds now, slowly and expensively, it owns the asset its future franchisees will run. That argument is real, and it is why the right contract here is a hybrid in disguise: buy the operated service now to stop the leak, with configuration and prompts documented as client property (Lesson 32) and a shadowing clause, so the capability transfers before the franchise launch. The framework gives a verdict; judgment gives the contract shape.
